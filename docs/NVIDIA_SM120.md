# NVIDIA sm_120 SASS in pure Sig

`src/nvidia/` holds Galaxy's way to make GPU kernels for NVIDIA's Blackwell GeForce GPUs (sm_120: the RTX 50 series, which includes the Legion 5's RTX 5070 Laptop GPU) without CUDA. It plays the role for NVIDIA that `src/adreno/` (ir3) plays for the Surface's Adreno:

- `sm120.sig`: the instruction set. One line of nvdisasm syntax, after its control field, becomes the instruction's 128 bits (`encode`); 128 bits become the instruction again (`decode`); an instruction prints as nvdisasm prints it (`format`).
- `sm120_asm.sig`: kernels. Labels, comments, `.params` and `.stack` headers and branches go in. Out come the code words, padded to 128-byte lines as ptxas pads, plus the register, stack and parameter counts a launch needs. Each instruction carries a control field, or none does, and then the assembler works the fields out.
- `sm120_sched.sig`: the control fields, worked out (below).
- `sm120_latency.sig`: NVIDIA's latency tables, as Mesa ships them. It is generated by `tools/gen_sm120_latencies.py`.

Nothing from NVIDIA is needed to build or test it. Galaxy launches its kernels on its own driver (galaxy `src/kernel/nv_chan.sig`).

## Where the encodings come from

- **One description per mnemonic.** Each mnemonic has a codec: a function that visits its fields in the order nvdisasm prints them. Encoding, it takes the operands and modifiers and puts their bits. Decoding, it reads the bits and appends the operands and modifiers, so a decoded instruction's modifiers come out in nvdisasm's order. `decode` then encodes what it read again and refuses (`error.Unknown`) a word with any bit it did not account for: what it returns is exactly the word.
- **Mesa's NAK compiler** (`src/nouveau/compiler/nak/sm70_encode.rs`, MIT) was read as documentation. It gave the ALU forms (bits 9..11), the operand slots, the predicates, the memory and constant-load fields, the branch offset, the conversions', shuffles' and MMAs' fields and the control bits; NAK covers sm_100 and sm_120. None of it is copied.
- **ptxas's own output** is the test data. `tools/gen_sm120_vectors.py` runs ptxas (`-arch=sm_120 -O3`), nvdisasm and cuobjdump from CUDA 13.4 on the PTX in `src/nvidia/testdata/`. It writes `sm120_vectors.txt`, with each instruction's words, its control field spelled out and its disassembly, plus each kernel's register, stack and parameter counts, its own label and its subroutines'. The PTX is shaped like the kernels to come:
  - `lfm3_quant.ptx`: Q4_K, Q6_K and Q8_0 rows dotted with Q8_K and Q8_0 activations (nibble and 6-bit unpacking, f16 scales, `dp4a`, warp shuffles), and Q8_0 quantization with an IEEE division;
  - `lfm3_layers.ptx`: RMSNorm through shuffles and shared memory, softmax attention over an f16 KV cache, NeoX RoPE, SwiGLU, the gated short convolution, residual adds;
  - `galaxy_draw.ptx`: alpha blending of RGBA8, a box blur through a shared-memory tile, integer and float conversions, 8- to 128-bit loads and stores;
  - `sm120_ops.ptx`: integer and warp operations, convergence, named barriers, packed halves, the uniform datapath, and the tensor cores' f16, bf16 and int8 MMAs.
- **What was read off the vectors,** where NAK doesn't emit a form or ptxas sets other bits:
  - the two-source `IADD` and `IADD.64`; `MOV.64` and `UMOV.64` (a 64-bit immediate in bits 24..87); `STL`'s 0x387 form;
  - `I2FP`, `F2IP`, `VIMNMX` (signedness in bit 72), `SEL.64` (0x607, `SEL`'s unused form 3);
  - `LDS`'s uniform register (in 32, present when bit 91 is set) and `STS`'s (0x988, in 64);
  - `BAR`'s barrier (54..57) and thread count (42..53, printed when not 0); `WARPSYNC`'s register (in 24);
  - `CALL.REL.NOINC` and `RET.REL.NODEC` (a branch's offset; `RET`'s to the kernel's start);
  - `.reuse` on `FADD`'s and `HADD2`'s second source is c's slot (124), wherever the source sits;
  - nvdisasm's names for operand patterns and its operands printed only when they're not `PT` or `RZ`.
- **nvdisasm on raw words.** nvdisasm also disassembles raw instruction words (`-b SM120`), so field values and forms ptxas never emits were checked against it too. `decode` and `format` were run on every vector's word with each of bits 9..104 flipped, and their text compared with nvdisasm's for each word `decode` accepted. What that found, and the families it led to, are in `testdata/sm120_raw.txt` (`gen_sm120_vectors.py --raw`), which the tests decode, print and encode again:
  - `IMAD`'s names for what it computes, signed or not: `IMAD.MOV` when a or b is zero, or b is 1 with no addend; else `IMAD.IADD` when b is 1; else `IMAD.SHL` when b is a power of two from 2 to 2^30 with no addend. There are none when c is an immediate, a constant or uniform, and none for `.WIDE` or `.HI`;
  - integer immediates print signed in `IMAD`, `ISETP` and `VIMNMX`, `.U32` or not (`ISETP.LE.U32.OR P1, PT, R9, -0x7ffffffd, P0`);
  - sm_120's ALU ops read no constants: nvdisasm calls every constant-buffer form (forms 3 and 5) illegal, and ptxas loads constants with `LDC` and `LDCU`. A uniform c (form 7) exists only for `IMAD` (all three), `FFMA`, `FADD`, `SHF`, `HADD2`, `HFMA2` and `F2IP`;
  - global memory's bits: 72 is `.E` (a 64-bit address); 76 picks `desc[URd][Ra.64+offset]`, else the uniform register is added, `[Ra.64+URb+offset]`; 84..86 the eviction priority (`EF`, `EL`, `LU`, `EU`, `NA`); 77..80 the order and scope (`CONSTANT`, `STRONG.SM`, `STRONG.GPU`, `STRONG.SYS`, `MMIO.GPU` and the `.PRIVATE` and `CTA`/`SM`/`VC` variants; a store's 4 and 13..15 are `STRONG.SM.PRIVATE` and the `SYS.ORDERED` ones). They print in that order, `LDG.E.EL.128.STRONG.GPU`;
  - `LDC`'s and `LDCU`'s offsets are signed fields (nvdisasm prints 0x837c as `-0x7c84`): only the non-negative ones are covered;
  - floats print `.FTZ`, then the rounding, then `.SAT` (`FFMA.FTZ.RM.SAT`), and `F2I` and `F2F` put `.FTZ` first too;
  - `.BF16_V2` halves' immediates are bfloat16s (`HFMA2.BF16_V2 R3, -RZ, RZ, 127, 0`), and it takes no `.F32`;
  - `I2FP` has only an f32 result from a 32-bit source; `I2F`'s byte selects are for byte sources only; `HADD2`'s a takes no `|..|`; `LDC` has no `.128`; `LEA.X`'s bit 72 is a NOT (`~R4`), not covered; `BAR.ARV` takes no `DEFER_BLOCKING`;
  - `UMOV`, `S2UR` and `LDCU` are on the uniform datapath (their guards uniform predicates); `R2UR` and `VOTEU` aren't;
  - floats' specials: `-0.0`, `+QNAN` and `-QNAN` (the quiet bit set), `+SNAN` and `-SNAN`, each followed by a space like `+INF`. A NaN's payload isn't printed: written, they read back as 0x7fc00000, 0xffc00000, 0x7f800001 and 0xff800001.

  After these, `decode` accepts 100,709 of the 208,128 mutations, each prints as nvdisasm prints it, and none of them is one nvdisasm calls illegal: the last few it was too lenient on (`IMMA.16864` of bytes, `F2F.F64.F64`, b's `.reuse` beside a uniform c) are refused now.

nvdisasm's text is reproduced exactly, with one exception: a branch's label (`.L_x_3`) is the kernel's numbering of its targets, which one instruction doesn't know, so `format` prints a decoded target as its address, `` `(0x1a0) ``, which parses back. Floats print as nvdisasm prints them: C's `%.20g` below 1e9, `%.20e` from there up, and `+INF ` and `+QNAN ` with nvdisasm's trailing space.

## The control fields

Every instruction carries its own scheduling above bit 104: the cycles to stall before the next instruction issues, the scoreboard (of six) its variable-latency work releases, and the scoreboards to wait on first. The hardware doesn't track register hazards. `sm120_sched.sig` works these fields out the way Mesa's NAK does (`calc_instr_deps.rs`, read as documentation):

- **Latency categories.** Each register an instruction reads or writes gets NAK's sm_120 category (`sm120_instr_latencies.rs`), in its file: general, uniform, predicate or uniform predicate. Fixed-latency ("coupled") results are waited for by stalling. Variable-latency ("decoupled") ones are waited for through scoreboards: memory (`LDG`, `STG`, `LDS`, `STS`, `LDL`, `STL`), constant loads, special registers, `SHFL`, `MUFU`, the conversions `F2I`, `I2F`, `F2F`, and `POPC`, `FLO`, `BREV`, `FCHK`. The uniform datapath's ops are NAK's `udp`. A guard predicate is read at issue. NAK has no category for some forms, so they get the nearest ones:
  - `IADD` as `IADD3`;
  - 64-bit results (`IADD.64`, `MOV.64`, `I2FP`) as a 64-bit dispatch's;
  - `UMOV.64` as `UMOV`, `VIMNMX` as `IMNMX`, `F2IP` as `F2FP`;
  - `FSEL` and `FCHK` as NAK's notes have them (ALU, decoupled);
  - `LOP3`'s predicate result as `PLOP3`'s;
  - `CALL` as a branch, and `WARPSYNC`'s and `RET`'s registers as a branch's reads.
- **The cycles for each hazard.** A read after a write, a write after a read, and a write after a write each take NVIDIA's cycles for the pair (Mesa's `latencies/sm100/*.csv`, which NAK uses for sm_120). Reads after writes get NAK's one cycle of sm_120 padding on top. The tensor cores' general registers get NAK's measured sm_120 adjustment instead, for every kind of hazard: 9 cycles for `HMMA`, 7 for `IMMA`. When the tables have no entry for a pair, the worst case is assumed.
- **Scoreboards.** When a pair needs one, the first instruction releases a scoreboard and the second waits on it, at least two cycles after it was set. A free scoreboard is used if there is one. Otherwise the one the most recent instruction holds is shared, and waiting on it then waits on both.
- **Exec latency.** `BAR` holds the next instruction back 6 cycles, as NAK has it (ptxas's `BAR.SYNC` stalls 6 too). NAK's extra NOP after it is left out.
- **Branches end a block** (`BRA`, `BRA.U`, `CALL`, `RET`), and so does an unconditional `EXIT` or `RET`. A branch waits on every scoreboard in flight, and stalls until every fixed-latency result before it is ready for any reader. A block therefore starts with nothing pending, whichever way it is entered, a `BSYNC`'s join included. NAK instead follows latencies across blocks with a dataflow pass. This approach is simpler, and costs a few cycles per loop iteration.
- **Long stalls.** A stall over 15 cycles is split with NOPs after the instruction, before the labels are placed.
- **What's left as written:** bit 109 stays clear, as NAK leaves it on sm_120, and `.reuse` stays as written.

`check` verifies a kernel's fields against the model independently. The tests take ptxas's 25 kernels and strip their fields, then schedule and check them, with nothing open. They also compare the result with ptxas's own fields:
- ptxas never treats a register read or write as variable latency where the model treats it as fixed. (A read scoreboard covers the registers a decoupled unit reads later, not the guard: ptxas rewrites a store's guard without waiting on the store's read scoreboard.)
- no hazard is closer than both ptxas has it and the model needs.

- it takes sm_100's latencies without NAK's padding (5 cycles where NAK has 6, 13 where NAK has 14);
- it relies on memory operations completing in order (no read scoreboard before a later load or address write);
- it carries scoreboards across a loop's back edge;
- it treats some ops NAK calls decoupled as fixed latency (`I2F.U8`, `BREV`, some `POPC` and `FLO`), and the MMAs as fixed with long stalls (the model agrees on the MMAs).

The model stays on the safe side of each.

### The graphics stages

A vertex and a pixel program need seven instructions beside the ALU's: `ALD`, `AST`, `IPA`, `PIXLD`, `KILL`, `TEX` and `TLD`. ptxas compiles compute kernels only, so there is no output of its to compare with. Their fields are NAK's (`sm70_encode.rs`), and their text is nvdisasm's reading of raw words, the same from CUDA 13.1 and 13.4. `testdata/sm120_gfx_raw.txt` holds 189 such words, with the `FMUL.RZ`, `MUFU.RCP` and `EXIT` a pixel program has beside them. The tests assemble each line's text to its word, control field included, then decode the word and print it as nvdisasm did. What goes in front of such a program is `sph.sig`'s header.

- **Attributes.** An address is in bytes: the position at 0x70..0x7c, generic vector n at 0x80 + 16n, the instance's and the vertex's index at 0x2f8 and 0x2fc.
  - Loads: `ALD R0, a[0x2fc]`, and two to four words into consecutive registers with `.64`, `.96` and `.128` (`ALD.128 R4, a[0x80]`). `.O` reads an output back. Another vertex's attributes take a vertex register last (`ALD.O R4, a[0x88], R4`). An index register goes with `.P`, patch attributes (`ALD.P R5, a[R7+0x20]`), and with `.PHYS`, a physical address (`ALD.PHYS R5, a[R7]`), and with nothing else.
  - Stores: `AST.128 a[0x70], R4`, the address first, then the first of the registers; `AST.64`, `AST.96`, `AST.P`, `AST.PHYS`, a vertex register last.
- **A pixel's inputs:** `IPA.PASS R0, a[0x7c]`, `IPA.CONSTANT R6, a[0x3fc]` and `IPA.STATE`, at the pixel's centre, at the centroid (`IPA.PASS.CENTROID R1, a[0x80]`) or at an offset in a register (`IPA.PASS.OFFSET R5, a[0x80], R9`). A predicate result is printed after the destination when there is one (`IPA.PASS R5, P0, a[0x80]`). Perspective isn't in the instruction. The header's map says how a word is carried, and the program multiplies by the reciprocal of `a[0x7c]`: `MUFU.RCP R0, R0`, then `FMUL.RZ R1, R1, R0`.
- **A pixel's samples:** `PIXLD R0` (their count), `PIXLD.COVMASK R0`, `.CENTROID_OFFSET`, `.MY_INDEX`, `.INNER_COVERAGE`, with a predicate result too (`PIXLD.COVMASK R5, P0`).
- **Discard:** `KILL`, `@P0 KILL` and `@!P0 KILL` (by the guard, as NAK writes it), or by its own predicate (`KILL P1`, `KILL !P0`).
- **Textures** are bindless: the handle is in a register.
  - `TEX.B.DXY R2, R0, R4, R6, 2D` is NAK's `texture()` on sm_120. The second destination is printed first (R2: blue and alpha), then the first (R0: red and green), the coordinates (R4, R5), the handle (R6) and the dimension: `1D`, `2D`, `3D`, `CUBE`, `ARRAY_1D`, `ARRAY_2D`, `ARRAY_CUBE`.
  - The channel mask is printed when it isn't all four, and the second destination is `RZ` when the mask leaves it none: `TEX.B.DXY RZ, R0, R4, R6, 2D, 0x3`. A fault predicate goes first when there is one (`TEX.B.DXY P0, R12, R10, R7, R9, 2D`), and a uniform register after the handle.
  - Modifiers print in this order: `.SCR`, `.B`, `.F16.RN` or `.F16.RZ`, the level of detail (`.LZ`, `.LB`, `.LL`, `.LC`, `.LB.LC`, and `.ULB`, `.ULL`, `.ULC`, `.ULB.LC`, `.LB.ULC`), `.AOFFI` or `.UAOFFI`, `.UAI`, `.DC`, the eviction priority (`.EF`, `.EL`, `.LU`, `.EU`, `.NA`), `.NDV` or `.DXY`, `.NODEP`. So `TEX.B.LZ R2, R0, R4, R6, 2D` and `TEX.B.DXY.NODEP R2, R0, R4, R6, 2D`.
  - A texel fetch is `TLD.B.LZ R2, R0, R4, R6, 2D`, with `.LL` or `.ULL` for the level, then the offsets, the eviction priority, `.MS` or `.UMS`, `.CL` and `.NODEP`. It has no cubes.
- **Refused,** because nvdisasm calls the word illegal or prints part of it as invalid: an index register without `.P` or `.PHYS`; `.PHYS` without one, with `.P`, or of more than one word; `.P` with a vertex register (`AST.P` takes one without an index); `IPA` at place 3 or frequency 3; `PIXLD`'s values 5 to 7; a texture source that is `RZ`; derivative mode 2, dimension 6, levels 11 to 15, eviction 6 and 7; `AST` with a write scoreboard, and `KILL` with any.
- **The control fields.** All seven are variable latency, in NAK's categories: attribute loads and stores, `IPA` and `PIXLD` as memory is (`decoupled_agu`), textures and `KILL` as `decoupled`, a texture's uniform register as `tex`. Three choices here go beyond what NAK says, each to the safe side:
  - a texture's sources count as the longest vector, four registers each (their real lengths depend on the dimension and the modes), and each destination as at least one register;
  - `KILL`'s own predicate, which NAK never uses, is read as a decoupled one, a predicate's worst case;
  - `AST` and `KILL` get no scoreboard they can't carry from the model itself: `AST` writes no register, and `KILL` touches none. `EXIT`, as every branch does, waits on what is still out, so the `EXIT` after the last store carries the stores' read scoreboards.

  The tests schedule a five-instruction vertex program and an eleven-instruction pixel program (two interpolations in perspective, a texture, a discard under its guard), check them against the model, and pin the result. nvdisasm takes every word of both.
- **Not covered:** the forms with a uniform index (`ALD` 0x521, `AST` 0x522, `IPA` 0x526) and `IPA`'s offset as an immediate (0x926); `AL2P`; the other texture ops (`TLD4`, `TXD`, `TXQ`, `TMML`) and the 0xf6x forms without `.B`; the geometry and tessellation stages' `OUT`, `ISBERD` and `ISBEWR`; `LDTRAM`. An instruction holds eight modifiers, so the few texture words nvdisasm prints with more (`TEX.SCR.B.F16.RN.LZ.AOFFI.DC.EF.DXY.NODEP`) aren't read. The emulator doesn't run any of these yet. Nothing here has run on the RTX.

### Graphics stages in the emulator

`sm120_emu.sig` runs a vertex and a pixel program, for the emulated 3D engine the driver is tested against before the RTX. `stageBegin` starts the threads (every register garbage: nothing is preloaded in either stage) and `stageRun` runs them to `EXIT` in warps of 32, with the timing checks a compute launch gets. A `Stage` carries what the engine gives the threads and takes back:

- **In:** a thread's attribute words. A vertex program loads them with `ALD` (the engine writes the vertex's index at word 0x2fc / 4, a vertex buffer's words where the streams put them). A pixel program reads them with `IPA`, already carried across the triangle by the engine as the header says.
- **Out:** the words a vertex program stores with `AST`, and a bit for each word a thread stored. A pixel program's colour is what R0..R3 hold at `EXIT` (`threadReg`), and `killed` says which pixels `KILL` discarded.
- **Constant buffers,** as a launch has them, and two callbacks into the engine: `sample` for `TEX` and `fetch` for `TLD`.

What each instruction does there:

- `ALD` and `AST` move one to four words between a thread's words and its registers. A vector starts where NAK starts one: two words at a multiple of 8, three or four at a multiple of 16.
- `IPA.PASS` and `IPA.CONSTANT` read the pixel's word at its centre. Nothing in the instruction multiplies it: perspective is the header's, and the program's own `FMUL.RZ` by `MUFU.RCP` of `a[0x7c]`.
- `KILL` marks the pixel discarded where its guard says. The thread runs on to its `EXIT`, as NAK has it (a demotion, not an end).
- `TEX.B` (with `.LZ`, `.NDV`, `.DXY`, `.NODEP`) calls `sample` with the handle and two float coordinates; `TLD.B.LZ` and `.LL` call `fetch` with the handle, two integers and the level. The channels the mask names go to registers in order: the first two from the first destination, the rest from the second. The level of detail and the derivatives are the engine's: the callback is given one pixel's coordinates.

With the program's header (`sph.sig`) in the Stage, the emulator holds the program to it, as `error.HeaderMismatch`:

- a word loaded or stored that the header doesn't declare, a store outside the range of stored words it gives, a load after a store when it says there is none, a discard it doesn't declare;
- a word read flat (`IPA.CONSTANT`) that the header carries across the triangle, or the other way;
- at the end, a word the header declares stored that a thread never stored, and an output register (a colour's, the sample mask's, the depth's) a pixel that wasn't discarded never wrote.

A header of the other kind, or a pixel header that doesn't read the position's w, is a bad launch.

The control fields are checked as a kernel's are. All of these set scoreboards, and nothing may read a result, or overwrite a register a store still reads, before a wait on the right one. A texture is held to the registers it really touches (two coordinates, the handle, a result register per channel), which are among the ones `sm120_sched` schedules for. One check is a stage's own: an `EXIT` must issue with no scoreboard still out, as NAK schedules it.

What it refuses, each with a sentence from `explain`:

- **Not this stage's** (`error.BadInstruction`): a graphics instruction in a compute launch; `BAR`, shared memory and the thread's or the CTA's special registers in a stage; `ALD` and `AST` in a pixel program; `IPA`, `PIXLD` and `KILL` in a vertex program.
- **No data in a Stage** (`error.Unsupported`): `PIXLD` in every form (a Stage says nothing of a pixel's samples), `IPA.CENTROID` and `IPA.OFFSET`, an attribute past 0x2fc (the front face, the barycentrics), local memory, a constant buffer past the eighth, a texture instruction without its callback.
- **Not known well enough to run** (`error.Unsupported`): `IPA.STATE`, a predicate result from `IPA`, `KILL` with its own predicate, `ALD.O`, the patch and physical forms, a vertex register, a vector NAK would have split, and of the textures every dimension but `2D`, a level from a register for `TEX`, texel offsets, `.DC`, `.SCR`, `.UAI`, the `.F16` pair, the eviction priorities, `.MS`, `.UMS`, `.CL`, a fault predicate and a uniform register.

Not checked: a register read before anything wrote it, the register count the engine programs for the stage, and whether vectors sit at the registers NAK aligns them to. None of this has been compared with the hardware.

## Writing kernels: the syntax

Kernels are written as nvdisasm prints, with or without control fields. These are the forms the tests check, spelled as ptxas emits them:

- **Shared memory:** `LDS.U8 R21, [R2+UR4+0x700]`, `LDS.128 R8, [UR4]`, `STS.64 [R12+0x400], R18`, `STS [UR4+0x20], R4`. A negative offset is `[R3+-0x10]`. ptxas makes a shared variable's address from the CTA's place in its cluster: `S2UR UR5, SR_CgaCtaId`, `UMOV UR4, 0x400`, `ULEA UR4, UR5, UR4, 0x18`.
- **Global memory:** `LDG.E.128.CONSTANT R4, desc[UR6][R4.64]` (`ld.global.nc`), `STG.E.U16 desc[UR4][R8.64], R10`; the descriptor from `LDCU.64 UR4, c[0x0][0x358]`. Eviction goes before the size, order after: `LDG.E.EL.128.STRONG.GPU`. Without a descriptor a uniform register is added: `LDG.E R18, [R14.64+UR4]`.
- **Constants:** `LDC R9, c[0x3][R9]`, `LDCU UR4, c[0x3][UR4]`, `LDC.U16 R11, c[0x0][0x3a4]`.
- **Shuffles:** `SHFL.BFLY PT, R0, R3, 0x4, 0x181f` (the in-range predicate first, then destination, source, lane, clamp; the clamp's high byte is the segment mask, `0x1f` for the whole warp), `SHFL.UP PT, R14, R12, 0x1, RZ`, `SHFL.IDX PT, R10, R6, RZ, 0x1f`.
- **The special-function unit:** `MUFU.EX2 R12, R9`, `MUFU.RCP R3, R0`, `MUFU.RSQ R15, R0`, `MUFU.LG2`, `MUFU.SQRT`, `MUFU.SIN` and `MUFU.COS` (of the angle in turns: ptxas multiplies by 1/2π first, `FMUL.RZ R17, R6, 0.15915493667125701904`).
- **Barriers and convergence:** `BAR.SYNC.DEFER_BLOCKING 0x0`, `BAR.SYNC.DEFER_BLOCKING 0x2, 0x40` (a thread count), `BAR.ARV 0x3, 0x60`; ``BSSY.RECONVERGENT B0, `(join)`` before a divergent region and `BSYNC.RECONVERGENT B0` at `join:`; `WARPSYNC R6`.
- **Halves:** `HADD2.F32 R12, -RZ, R4.H0_H0` widens a half (`.H1_H1` the high one), `HFMA2 R0, R4, 1, 1, R5` takes an immediate as two halves (high first), `F2FP.F16.F32.PACK_AB R7, R14, R3` packs two (R14 into the high half).
- **Integer dot products:** `IDP.4A.S8.S8 R9, R9, R20, RZ`, `IDP.4A.U8.S8` (unsigned a: Q4_K's nibbles).
- **Operands printed only when they mean something:** `LOP3.LUT P0, RZ, R9, 0x7, RZ, 0xc0, !PT` (a zero test into P0), `LEA R8, P0, R4, R9, 0x3` (a carry out) and `LEA.HI.X R9, R4, R3, R5, 0x3, P0` (in), `VOTE.ANY R21, PT, P1` (the ballot) or `VOTE.ANY P3, P1`, ``@!P1 BRA !P2, `(target)``.
- **Uniform guards:** the uniform datapath's ops take uniform predicates, `@!UP0 UIADD3 UR5, UPT, UPT, UR4, 0x1, URZ`; ``BRA.U !UP0, `(target)``.
- **Tensor cores:** `HMMA.16816.F32 R12, R4, R2, R16` (a four registers, b two, c and d four), `IMMA.16832.S8.S8 R20, R4.ROW, R2.COL, R8` (the layouts are printed; they may be left out when writing).
- **Subroutines:** ``CALL.REL.NOINC `(name)`` with the return address set first (`MOV R12, 0x240`), and ``RET.REL.NODEC R12 `(kernel)`` (no comma), the kernel's own label at its start.
- **Branch targets:** a label, `` `(.L_x_0) ``, or an address, `` `(0x1a0) ``.
