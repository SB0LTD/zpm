# NVIDIA sm_120 SASS in pure Sig

`src/nvidia/` holds Galaxy's way to make GPU kernels for NVIDIA's Blackwell GeForce GPUs (sm_120: the RTX 50 series, which includes the Legion 5's RTX 5070 Laptop GPU) without CUDA. It plays the role for NVIDIA that `src/adreno/` (ir3) plays for the Surface's Adreno:

- `sm120.sig`: the instruction set. One line of nvdisasm syntax, after its control field, becomes the instruction's 128 bits.
- `sm120_asm.sig`: kernels. Labels, comments, `.params` and `.stack` headers and branches go in. Out come the code words, padded to 128-byte lines as ptxas pads, plus the register, stack and parameter counts a launch needs. Each instruction carries a control field, or none does, and then the assembler works the fields out.
- `sm120_sched.sig`: the control fields, worked out (below).
- `sm120_latency.sig`: NVIDIA's latency tables, as Mesa ships them. It is generated by `tools/gen_sm120_latencies.py`.

Nothing from NVIDIA is needed to build or test it. Galaxy launches its kernels on its own driver (galaxy `src/kernel/nv_chan.sig`).

## Where the encodings come from

- **Mesa's NAK compiler** (`src/nouveau/compiler/nak/sm70_encode.rs`, MIT) was read as documentation. It gave the ALU forms (bits 9..11), the operand slots, the predicates, the memory and constant-load fields, the branch offset and the control bits, and NAK covers sm_100 and sm_120. None of it is copied.
- **ptxas's own output** is the test data. `tools/gen_sm120_vectors.py` runs ptxas (`-arch=sm_120 -O3`), nvdisasm and cuobjdump from CUDA 13.4 on the PTX in `src/nvidia/testdata/`. It writes `sm120_vectors.txt`, with each instruction's words, its control field spelled out and its disassembly, plus each kernel's register, stack and parameter counts. The forms NAK doesn't emit were read off these vectors, and so were the constant bits ptxas sets:
  - the two-source `IADD` and `IADD.64`;
  - `MOV.64` and `UMOV.64` (a 64-bit immediate in bits 24..87);
  - `STL`'s 0x387 form;
  - `I2FP`.

## The control fields

Every instruction carries its own scheduling above bit 104: the cycles to stall before the next instruction issues, the scoreboard (of six) its variable-latency work releases, and the scoreboards to wait on first. The hardware doesn't track register hazards. `sm120_sched.sig` works these fields out the way Mesa's NAK does (`calc_instr_deps.rs`, read as documentation):

- **Latency categories.** Each register an instruction reads or writes gets NAK's sm_120 category (`sm120_instr_latencies.rs`). Fixed-latency ("coupled") results are waited for by stalling. Variable-latency ("decoupled") ones, from loads, constant loads and special registers, are waited for through scoreboards. NAK has no `IADD`, `MOV.64`, `UMOV.64` or `I2FP`, so they get the nearest categories:
  - `IADD` as `IADD3`;
  - 64-bit results as a 64-bit dispatch's;
  - `UMOV.64` as `UMOV`.
- **The cycles for each hazard.** A read after a write, a write after a read, and a write after a write each take NVIDIA's cycles for the pair (Mesa's `latencies/sm100/*.csv`, which NAK uses for sm_120). Reads after writes get NAK's one cycle of sm_120 padding on top. When the tables have no entry for a pair, the worst case is assumed.
- **Scoreboards.** When a pair needs one, the first instruction releases a scoreboard and the second waits on it, at least two cycles after it was set. A free scoreboard is used if there is one. Otherwise the one the most recent instruction holds is shared, and waiting on it then waits on both.
- **Branches end a block, and so does an unconditional `EXIT`.** A branch waits on every scoreboard in flight, and stalls until every fixed-latency result before it is ready for any reader. A block therefore starts with nothing pending, whichever way it is entered. NAK instead follows latencies across blocks with a dataflow pass. This approach is simpler, and costs a few cycles per loop iteration.
- **Long stalls.** A stall over 15 cycles is split with NOPs after the instruction, before the labels are placed.
- **What's left as written:** bit 109 stays clear, as NAK leaves it on sm_120, and `.reuse` stays as written.

`check` verifies a kernel's fields against the model independently. The tests take ptxas's six kernels and strip their fields, then schedule and check them, with nothing open. They also compare the result with ptxas's own fields:
- ptxas never treats an instruction's results as variable latency where the model treats them as fixed;
- no hazard is closer than both ptxas has it and the model needs.

| kernel | instructions | hazards | cycles in order (this / ptxas) | waits (this / ptxas) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| galaxy_m4 | 9 | 13 | 20 / 21 | 2 / 2 |
| galaxy_local | 104 | 264 | 142 / 180 | 14 / 11 |
| galaxy_matmul | 81 | 228 | 229 / 292 | 19 / 21 |
| galaxy_copy | 24 | 59 | 70 / 104 | 8 / 8 |
| galaxy_fill | 22 | 49 | 54 / 76 | 7 / 7 |
| cov_imm | 18 | 42 | 24 / 32 | 3 / 3 |

ptxas spends more cycles on issue throughput: back-to-back loads and constant loads are spaced out. Those stalls aren't hazards, and NAK doesn't add them either.

ptxas is also tighter than the model in a few places:
- it takes sm_100's latencies without NAK's padding (5 cycles where NAK has 6, 13 where NAK has 14);
- it relies on memory operations completing in order (no read scoreboard before a later load or address write);
- it carries scoreboards across a loop's back edge.

The model stays on the safe side of each.

## What is covered

- **The tests (`sig build test-nvidia`):**
  - All 328 vectors encode to ptxas's 128 bits.
  - The six kernels (Galaxy's five probe kernels, M4 and M5's fill, copy, matmul and local, plus a coverage kernel) assemble from their listings to ptxas's exact words, registers, stack and parameter bytes. These are the words Galaxy runs on the RTX today (galaxy `nv_kernels.sig`).
  - The same six, with their fields removed, are scheduled and checked against the model and against ptxas (above). M4, written with no fields, assembles to ptxas's bits below the control field.
- **The forms:**
  - Moves and integer arithmetic: `MOV`, `MOV.64`, `UMOV.64`, `IADD`, `IADD.64`, `IADD3`, `IMAD`, `IMAD.WIDE`, `ISETP`, `LOP3.LUT`, `PLOP3.LUT`.
  - Floating point and conversion: `FFMA`, `FADD`, `HFMA2`, `I2FP`.
  - Special and uniform registers: `S2R`, `S2UR`, `R2UR`.
  - Constant, global and local memory: `LDC`, `LDCU`, `LDG`, `STG`, `LDL`, `STL`.
  - Control flow: `BRA`, `EXIT`, `NOP`.
- **Written from NAK, with no vector yet:** `FMUL`.
